CP-V5 — LEICA-2 VELODYNE BELIEF / COVARIANCE AUDIT
COMPLETED 2026-10-24 — SAVED DATA ONLY, NO TUNING
================================================

Conclusion
----------
Yes, the covariance contains useful information. GLIM is not universally
orders of magnitude more confident than Kimera. But relative covariance scale,
within-source health ranking, and actual optimizer influence are three distinct
questions. This sequence gives particularly strong evidence to examine the
ROTATIONAL component of GLIM -> Kimera coupling separately from translation.

At1905 actual installed GLIM -> Kimera factors, using identical receiver
endpoints and verified pre-update Kimera means:
- Incoming GLIM rotation is worse than Kimera's pre-state on83.67% of edges.
- Incoming GLIM rotational covariance trace is larger on84.72% of edges.
- Nevertheless, choosing whichever has the smaller rotational trace gives
  0.17685deg error RMS, versus0.14035deg keeping Kimera's pre-state:26.0% worse.
- Incoming GLIM rotational trace/error Spearman correlation is only0.047.
  A larger covariance on average is not a reliable per-belief health detector.
- Translation is different: the same offline smaller-trace choice gives
  12.486mm RMS versus13.786mm for Kimera's pre-state:9.4% lower.

These are selection diagnostics on already recorded means, NOT a newly solved
trajectory, a proposed hard switch, or measured improvement from changing CBS.
No coefficient is fitted and no factor removed. The evidence supports one
future controlled G2K rotational-weight sensitivity test; it does NOT justify
globally discarding GLIM, a universal scalar, or promoting a new setting.

1. Correct time/mean/covariance comparison
----------------------------------------
For each original source interval[a,b], compute its own reference relative
motion Zref=Tref(a)^-1*Tref(b), then e=Log(Zbelief^-1*Zref). Rotation-first,
right-local6D tangent. Translation results below use ||rho||, not whole-path
ATE; Cartesian translation differences are also retained. No absolute-path
SE3 alignment is needed for these body-relative comparisons.

Reference interpolation uses position-linear/SO(3) interpolation at the exact
endpoint timestamps, never extrapolation. Maximum used bracket5ms. Export vs
native timestamp rounding <=387ns. This is NOT an interpolation of covariance:
the covariance is the actual matrix of that exact recorded belief version.

4. What was actually installed into Kimera
-----------------------------------------
All1905 G2K factors matched their exact exported revision0 mean/full6x6
covariance, receiver matrix, frozen mean and tracked temporary slot. Covariance
and frozen-mean differences are zero. G2K source endpoints are shifted back
~33.6ms to the actual receiver endpoints by the frozen mean-only alignment.
Score the logged aligned mean at those receiver timestamps, not a guessed
bridge covariance. Covariance remains unchanged; bridge uncertainty and its
transport are still the known approximation, not repaired by this audit.

All1905 Kimera pre-states were reconstructed from the immediately preceding
recorded MAIN revision snapshot, on the same receiver edge, and verified against
the actual pre-injection residual. Forty explicit pre-state matrix samples
provide an additional check. Exact same-epoch outgoing LOCAL covariance joins
exist for those pre-states. These are not the sum/information of all optimizer
factors, and lower trace is not a measure of the factor's actual leverage.

SAME receiver intervals, CBS ON:
                                 Translation RMS    Rotation RMS
Incoming aligned GLIM                 13.789mm          .32325deg
Kimera pre-update                     13.786mm          .14035deg
Offline smaller-trace choice          12.486mm          .17685deg
Offline perfect-error choice          10.022mm          .13212deg
Actual Kimera post-update             11.357mm          .14986deg

Perfect-error choice uses reference hindsight only as a diagnostic lower
benchmark; it is not implementable online. Smaller-trace choice uses the two
already recorded means independently per block; it is NOT a physically fused
SE3 trajectory or a counterfactual estimator run. Do not interpret the9.4%
translation reduction or26.0% rotation increase as actual fusion-run changes.

Incoming GLIM is more accurate on40.73% translation /16.33% rotation edges.
It reports lower trace on50.08% /15.28%. Smaller-trace selection picks the
better candidate on61.89% /74.49%. The rotational74.49% is NOT a success:
always keeping Kimera would already be correct83.67% of the time. Of291
rotational choices favouring GLIM,233 select the worse rotation and58 the
better one. Cost of wrong choices matters, not just count.

Incoming GLIM reported RMS norm is2.261mm/.02459deg versus observed
13.789mm/.32325deg. Kimera pre LOCAL RMS norm is2.738mm/.01132deg.
Both are overconfident RELATIVE TO THIS REFERENCE; no true NEES/NIS or
calibrated probabilities follow because reference/model/cross-correlation
uncertainty is unavailable. Full-matrix reference-normalized discrepancies
are retained; trace alone is not sufficient for calibration or influence.

Before/after evidence, not causal attribution:
- Translation error improves on70.18% of these updates, overall RMS down17.6%.
- Rotation error worsens on59.06%, overall RMS up6.77%.
- On58.32% of all edges, post rotation is simultaneously closer to the incoming
  GLIM rotation and farther from the reference than the pre-state.
- The rotation effect is not confined to startup: moving-only post-worse
  fraction61.35%, and rotation RMS increases in each populated30s bin through
  390s. Example at155.399s (receiver763->764): pre.11756deg, incoming.83492deg,
  post.33215deg. Incoming/pre norm scales.01460/.01074deg. Another at26.9997s:
  pre.09477deg, incoming.57007deg, post.29702deg; incoming trace is smaller.

Each optimizer update also adds/updates native factors, handles retirement and
prior refresh, and inherits earlier CBS history. It is not an isolated injection
ablation. These observations SUPPORT investigating rotational influence; they
do not prove CBS alone caused each change or quantify how much of the full-path
ATE degradation is explained. Better immediate translation relative error can
coexist with worse long-trajectory drift/orientation; never equate them.

5. Opposite direction and alignment limitations
----------------------------------------------
All1913 K2G factors matched exported payloads and tracked slots:1905 revision0
and8 revision1. Classic temporary insertion need not always consume revision0;
this is distinct from live replacement of an installed source factor. The
initial analyzer assumption was corrected; exact evidence in RUN_NOTES.txt.

Kimera -> GLIM raw-source/aligned-receiver translation RMS13.757/16.867mm;
rotation.14006/.14137deg. Raw and aligned have different endpoints, so their
difference alone is not an alignment-effect estimate. Same-receiver negative
control (raw mean without alignment) gives15.562mm/.60025deg. Alignment helps
rotation strongly; translation is8.4% worse than that negative control in this
sample. Do not turn this into a proposal to disable alignment. These are logged
means, not a newly constructed bridge or transported covariance.

K2G translation trace/error rank falls from.636 raw-source to.247 aligned;
rotational rank remains~.294. Covariance magnitude alone misses the extra
alignment uncertainty. K2G receiver endpoints have median shift~-66.3ms,
range approximately-69.4..+37.1ms. No explicit GLIM pre-state matrices were
retained by this logging path; no invented full pre/post K2G comparison.
Thus the symmetric causal question for GLIM is not answered by these logs.
