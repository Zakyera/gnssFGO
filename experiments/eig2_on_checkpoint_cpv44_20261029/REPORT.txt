BOTTOM LINE
===========
We localized an early instability mechanism, NOT a proven specific software bug.
The ordinary CBS solve makes large coherent window-tilt changes while changing
accelerometer bias in almost exactly the direction/magnitude that compensates
the changed gravity projection. This is not merely Rerun drawing an old point.
The extra CP-V40 refresh generally repairs much of the resulting IMU residual,
but does not reliably prevent window motion and sometimes adds substantial tilt.

In these five windows, the ordinary solve already uses current initialized
states as its linearization point. Fresh native equations equal the saved cache
exactly, and independent direct solutions closely reproduce the live states.
Thus the early ON anomaly is NOT explained by the previously identified OFF
retained-linearization mechanism, a stale linear cache, or inaccurate linear
back-substitution in the tested windows. A correct linear solve is NOT a
nonlinear convergence certificate or proof that the graph is well constrained.

The strongest remaining lead is the coupled orientation/velocity/accelerometer-
bias response of the native graph with CBS constraints, including nonlinear
convergence and constraint consistency. No conclusion about covariance scale
is drawn. No full sequence or production fix is justified by this diagnostic.

3. GRAVITY / ACCELEROMETER-BIAS COUPLING
======================================
Serialized PIM gravity is [0,0,+9.81], exactly matching the frozen CPT7 profile.
The sign is NOT newly changed or diagnosed as wrong. The profile explains the
stationary CPT7 negative-Z specific-force convention.

For body specific force f=R^T(a-g)+b, a common rotation of trajectory pose and
world acceleration can be compensated approximately by
    delta_b = R_after^T g - R_before^T g.
This identity is evaluated on saved states, not fitted by adjusting weights.
It is conditional on common rotation of trajectory kinematics, not a proof of
an exact graph nullspace or physical sensor-bias changes.
