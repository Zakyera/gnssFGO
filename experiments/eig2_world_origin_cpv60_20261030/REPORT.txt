CP-V60 — Does the arbitrary world origin change Kimera's response?
2026-10-30 | EIG-2 Hesai | saved CP51 first CBS admission | suspect1

OUTCOME
YES: the native smart-factor triangulation/rejection path is coordinate
dependent, and that dependence changes the native refresh solution. This is
a demonstrated numerical-consistency problem, not a camera/LiDAR extrinsic
conversion mistake. It is NOT proof that this problem causes the full-run
zigzag, and no fix has been implemented or promoted.

At the tested event, initial warm caches make the first ordinary solve
effectively invariant. The difference appears when retriangulation runs for
refresh: nine previously valid visual factors are lost solely because of the
arbitrary coordinate translation. The final solutions differ by at most
0.158546mm and0.00565807deg across all seven poses. The much larger original
early rotation/bias disturbance remains in both representations.

CONCLUSION AND NEXT DECISION
This is a real coordinate-consistency defect/property in native algebraic
triangulation/rank/rejection, demonstrably influencing the refresh graph.
It is not evidence of wrong GLIM-to-Kimera body/extrinsic conversion.
The initial ordinary response is invariant, and the early .32deg rotational
revision remains in both representations. This test therefore does NOT show
that origin dependence explains the main first-event response. Accumulation
over later updates and the full trajectory remains untested.

The next narrowly scoped candidate, only if authorized, is triangulation in a
deterministic track-local camera frame, then transforming the landmark back.
First demand coordinate-invariance and exact-projection fixtures with unchanged
physical measurements/weights and explicit original rejection tests. Then test
the same saved ordinary+refresh event against the original, with any changed
valid sets disclosed. This is NOT just choosing a more favorable world origin,
not covariance tuning, not an implemented fix and not a full-run launch.
Stay on suspect1; do not silently move to suspect2.
