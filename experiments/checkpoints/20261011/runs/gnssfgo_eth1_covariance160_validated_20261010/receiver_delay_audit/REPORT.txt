ETH1 receiver delay: saved-log and code investigation, 2026-10-10
================================================================

Conclusion
----------
The latest delay was predominantly the experimental receiver's full-history
publication refinement, not an explosion in incoming belief count and not
Rerun rendering. The earlier GLIM diagnostic overhead was a separate problem;
its correction worked over the observed interval. The new receiver failure has
TWO parts: excessive work per publication and a numerical stationarity failure.
Faster evaluation can address the first without necessarily resolving the second.

No source estimator, optimizer replay, or new experiment was run for this audit.
No fusion/source code, settings, covariance, iteration limit or tolerance changed.
Only analyze.py, evidence.json and this report were added in this new directory.
167 previously protected inputs/artifacts still match their saved SHA-256 values.

1. What delayed the run
-----------------------
The preceding GLIM correction reduced mean scan processing 937.432 -> 517.860 ms
on the same 1143 timestamps. The principal improvement was in the post-update
callback (633.604 -> 205.094 ms); native smoother time stayed ~171 ms. The earlier
missing-history alignment failure did not recur. This is observed-interval
evidence, not certification of the unobserved end of the requested 160 s.

The corrected ON capture then encountered the following receiver costs:

For 120-125 s the measured ordinary revision-update totals were only 4.875 s
OFF and 6.147 s ON. Thus this was not chiefly a new avalanche of revision
messages: ON had slightly fewer versions in that interval, but much more
publication-refinement work. The normal replacement timer does not include
factor construction, pose queries, logging or waiting; these are not a complete
CPU profile. Publication totals are binned by physical support time; revision
totals by observed arrival time. They compare the same labelled sections, not
perfectly coincident exclusive CPU-time partitions.

At 0.10x playback each 100 ms support supplies 1 wall second for ALL processing.
The 50 supports in 120-125 s supply about 50 wall seconds; publication refinement
alone consumed 90.079 seconds. That is enough to demonstrate overload regardless
of the untimed work. It also excludes publication-log writing itself.

First recorded queue-gate breach: input elapsed 120.910027 s, 1050 queued events,
output age 1.830027 sensor seconds. Peak transport queue was 4096; maximum
sampled output age was 6.580029 sensor seconds. The 1000-event limit is a
validation gate, NOT the adapter's 200000-event overflow limit. This was not a
queue overflow, OOM, GPU crash, or visualization failure.

-------------------------------------------------
This is our experimental numerical-publication wrapper around GTSAM, not just
the normal cost of adding a factor to gnssFGO/iSAM2.

Retained FullPublicationRefinement.h in the same directory:

   freshly linearized gradient, factor-slot checks and state-step calculation;

   full QR rebuild, four GaussianCostChange calls, two normalized gradients,
   two objective evaluations and consistency checks for each remaining pass.

Repository helpers under /home/yeranis/repos/gnssFGO/online_fgo/include/offline_process/:

   at the supplied current Values; it does not reuse iSAM2's cached Jacobians.
 - StableGaussianCost.h:46-73: every GaussianCostChange call again linearizes
   the entire graph at its before-Values, then evaluates both residual sets.
 - TransactionalISAM2Rebuild.h:19-50: a rebuild bulk-loads the entire unchanged

The first multi-pass private polish ON appeared at support 1168, elapsed
117.38 s (2.524 wall s). Earlier ON QR recovery first appeared at 56.78 s, but
was short. In 120-125 s, 13/50 ON supports needed QR recovery, with 47 rebuilds;
OFF had 1/50 and one rebuild. OFF never entered multi-pass private polish in

At failed support 1272 there were 1273 support states / 3819 X,V,W keys and
3155 active factors (1272 WNOA, 1880 source IDs, three priors). Every ordinary
attempt logged 3819 relinearized variables. These are keys, not 3819 poses.
The factor graph was growing normally; 75121 processed revision records did
NOT mean 75121 simultaneously active source factors.

The failure's logged branches imply the following operation counts:
 20 ISAM2 candidate copies/updates;
 16 fresh full-history QR rebuilds;
 37 NormalizedGradient calls;
 62 GaussianCostChange calls;
 therefore 99 explicit full-graph linearizations OUTSIDE solver updates.
These are control-flow counts, NOT measured wall-time attribution to QR/copy/J.
The 15 inner-polish passes each invoke four cost comparisons and two gradients.

Another multiplier: current WNOA uses numerical derivatives. The historical
name useAutoDiff=true is NOT automatic differentiation. GPWNOAPrior.h:90-122
and GPWNOAInterpolator.h:92-122 use finite differences with step 1e-5.
SourceWnoaModel.h:64-65 selects this interpolator; DiagnosticReplay.cpp:1790
selects this prior. Full six-block prior linearization invokes the underlying
residual 55 times (24 central-difference directions, six baseline evaluations,
one returned residual), based on the pinned runtime's numericalDerivative11.
Repeated whole-graph differentiation is consequently substantial work.

3. Why it stopped, distinct from why it was slow
----------------------------------------------
At elapsed 127.78 s, support 1272 took 17.336845 wall seconds and exhausted
20 attempts, with only four accepted. Retained normalized gradient:
 1.4269950970074356e-5; unchanged publication limit: 1e-5.
The failed support was correctly withheld from online_trajectory.tum.

Gradient fell 1.380954 -> .00362226 -> .000276748 -> 2.42536e-5 -> 1.42700e-5
over initialization and the first four accepted refinement steps. Later
candidates oscillated above threshold; NONE of the logged trial gradients
passed it (smallest 1.13731e-5). The first rejected step had computed actual
cost change +2.219e-8 but predicted change -2.008e-8, on total objective
~12,295,403. This is an extremely flat regime at aggregate-cost precision.
Residual-wise stable cost comparison is already present; simply adding it is
not a new fix. Its arithmetic bound deliberately does not bound errors in
SE(3) residual evaluation, interpolation, whitening or numerical Jacobians.

For all 15 final private passes the recorded incremental/fresh-QR disagreement
was zero and their gradients matched, yet neither passed stationarity. Thus
rebuilding QR repeatedly did not resolve this instance. It is consistent with
a numerical derivative/evaluation/conditioning limit, but this audit does NOT
prove which of those is responsible. Nor does it prove covariance corruption,
physical divergence, or that the WNOA model is invalid.

The source group was deliberately interrupted near input 130.28 s after backlog
growth. The receiver then reached its own earlier physical failure while
draining captured input. Broken pipe and truncated-source messages were
secondary consequences, not the first bottleneck.

4. Applicable engineering work, in priority order (NOT implemented)
------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Remove redundant exact-point evaluation in the publication wrapper.
   This is the lowest-risk first candidate. Cache immutable Values snapshots,
   residuals and linearizations keyed by BOTH exact Values and factor identity/
   revision; preserve factor traversal/reduction order and all acceptance tests.
   In private polish, the anchor is fixed. We currently linearize that same
   anchor twice per pass solely to calculate predicted quantities subsequently
   discarded by CumulativeCostWithLocalPrediction. Split actual cost change
   from local predicted change. Reuse one seed linearization for both candidate
   predictions. This could remove 45 of the 60 cost-comparison linearizations
   in the failure's 15 inner passes (30 discarded anchor predictions plus
   15 duplicate seed linearizations), before considering other reuse.
   This count is an identified opportunity, not a measured speedup.
   Never replace a fresh-at-current-Values gradient with stale iSAM2 Jacobians.

B. Reuse the existing gnssFGO WNOA derivative infrastructure, only after tests.
   The library has an analytic/hybrid branch; even that branch contains a
   numerical derivative of the inverse right Jacobian. Do NOT blindly flip
   useAutoDiff=false. Verify it against independent derivatives of the exact
   current residual at real difficult snapshots and nontrivial synthetic states,
   including near-zero rotation and shared endpoint keys. An earlier full-WNOJ
   analytic branch failed a test; that does not prove WNOA fails, but rules out
   treating an existing branch as automatically certified. Validated derivative
   reuse/correction could improve both cost and gradient reliability without
   changing the physical model or its uncertainty. No claim of such success yet.

C. Reduce nonessential bookkeeping/IO after the numerical path is understood.
   Reuse one full estimate where multiple same-point calculateEstimate calls
   are made; maintain exact active-factor counters rather than repeatedly
   scanning the graph; buffer progress-file writes with crash-safe flushing.
   Keep source revision export intact. The multi-million-line GPU warning
   stream is another source-side IO candidate, but it cannot explain the
   receiver's demonstrated refinement time by itself. None is the primary
   demonstrated failure fix, and component-level timing is still needed.

Not a semantics-preserving quick fix: skipping strict convergence checks,
raising iteration/gradient limits, slowing playback and calling it solved,
dropping/coalescing revisions, batching replacements across publication
boundaries, shortening graph history, changing support cadence, modifying
covariance/Qc or priors. Some are separate design choices; none is authorized
or justified as the first correction here. Faster code alone would still
exhaust 20 iterations at the same nonstationary point if arithmetic is unchanged.
