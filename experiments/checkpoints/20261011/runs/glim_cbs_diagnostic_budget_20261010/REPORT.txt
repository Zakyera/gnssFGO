Mean scan processing decreased by about 45.3% (1.83x processing throughput).
In the difficult [60,80) second section it decreased from 1167.366 to 648.992 ms.
At 10 Hz LiDAR and 0.1x playback, the average budget is about 1000 wall ms/scan.
There are still isolated slow scans (maximum 1243 ms in the matched window),
but they did not accumulate into a backlog. Input workload peaked at 2 rather
than the previous ON peak of 11. This does not certify 1x or faster playback.

The comparison uses the exact same scan timestamp lists and frozen settings,
not identical asynchronous source factor-arrival histories. Faster execution
changes which information is available at an update; native trajectories are
not claimed to be byte-identical between old and new live source runs.

WHAT CHANGED
Owner: src/glim_ros1, cbs_bridge.cpp / cbs_bridge.hpp.
New helper: include/glim_ros/cbs_diagnostic_budget.hpp.
New focused test: test/cbs_diagnostic_budget_test.cpp; CMake test registration.
Documentation: src/glim_ros1/docs/CBS_ACTIVE_FACTOR_DIAGNOSTICS.txt.

The old active-factor detail-log switch only controlled PRINTING. The bridge
still queried factor errors/Hessians for every active CBS edge and its graph
neighbors, repeatedly triggering synchronous GPU work. The preceding retained
audit counted 1,143,005 successful neighbor-Hessian queries during partial ON.

The expensive diagnostic body is now gated BEFORE calculateEstimate(),
factor.error(), and factor.linearize(). Parameters:
  glim_cbs_active_factor_diagnostics_enable=false
  glim_cbs_active_factor_diagnostics_stride=20
  glim_cbs_active_factor_diagnostics_max_updates=40
When explicitly enabled, only every stride-th eligible update is sampled,
with a finite maximum number of attempts. Zero/negative maximum disables it.
Active factor lifetime counters still advance independently of diagnostics.
A separate timing row measures the diagnostic call, including its fast return.

In the new run: 991 calls, zero expensive diagnostic samples/rows, mean
diagnostic-call overhead 0.002158 ms (about 2.16 microseconds).
The existing separate incoming/local covariance competition audit remains ON:
25 GLIM samples, 24 Kimera samples. Full revision exports remain ON.

LIFECYCLE CHECKER LIMITATION FOUND AND RESOLVED
The original read-only checker failed at Kimera edge x433->x434 because it
counted a scheduled removal before a rollback and its retry as two committed
removals. Original failure is preserved in validation_logs/failed.txt and
lifecycle.log. No estimator was retried or patched to resolve this.

BPSAM::update snapshots itself before staging/removing factors, restores that
snapshot on staged_residual_regression, and retries without the staged CBS
factor (src/cbs/src/bpsam/bpsam.cpp:3654-3718,4590-4610).
The supplemental checker now restores its accounting snapshot at the matching
logged rollback, validating each slot and active-count transition. All five
transactions reconcile. It does NOT ignore duplicate removals arbitrarily.

Actual counts after rollback accounting:
  GLIM: 481 queued, 456 removed, 25 remaining.
  Kimera: 480 attempted registrations, five rolled back, 475 committed;
          455 committed removals, 20 remaining.
Five Kimera staged proposals were rejected by the EXISTING guard; that is not
a new missing-history or transport failure. Do not describe this mode as having
no internal safeguards, or claim zero inherited CBS information from counts alone.
These observations are retained for subsequent algorithmic interpretation.

REMAINING ENGINEERING OPPORTUNITY
In the matched window the unchanged sidecar update costs about 125 ms/scan and
LOCAL marginal reconstruction about 165 ms/scan. The local codebase already
has batched GPU linearization hooks in gtsam_points::ISAM2Ext, while BPSAM
inherits generic gtsam::ISAM2 and rebuilds generic gtsam::Marginals.
Reusing batching at exactly the same Values is a plausible next optimization,
but requires explicit Jacobian/Hessian/covariance equivalence tests. It was
not necessary for this 0.1x retest and was NOT implemented. Do not remove the
sidecar, use stale marginals, or mute warnings and call that equivalent.

CONCLUSION
The repeated diagnostic calculations were the main removable throughput
bottleneck. This bounded test supports retrying the originally intended 160 s
experiment using the corrected build, but does not itself certify that longer
window or establish any OFF/ON accuracy conclusion. No further run launched.
