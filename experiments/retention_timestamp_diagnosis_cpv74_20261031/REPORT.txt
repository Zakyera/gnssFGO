3. WHY CAN KEYFRAME TIMES DIFFER?
Kimera does not force one backend state on every fixed0.20s grid. Its frontend
uses elapsed time, tracked features and image disparity to select keyframes.
The saved shared profile sets min interval0.19s, max interval5s, disparity
threshold0.5px and IMU-rotation-assisted optical-flow prediction. Backend bias
updates feed back to the frontend's IMU prediction. A changed backend history
can therefore affect tracking and later keyframe decisions.

This is a source-supported mechanism, NOT proof of the exact trigger here.
Nearby LOW_DISPARITY/native no-motion-factor events differ between the two
arms, including REMOVE1551 versus RETAIN1553. The raw bag contains BOTH stereo
images at the expected1554 time AND both at RETAIN's selected later time, with
identical header/record stamps and consecutive camera seq10456/10457.
There is no raw stereo gap at these timestamps.

However, raw availability does not prove runtime subscriber delivery. The
per-frame visual-health audit and verbose keyframe-reason logs were not enabled.
Saved evidence cannot uniquely distinguish adaptive selection, tracking/IMU-
bias feedback, or runtime image loss at this event. No explicit dropped-image
warning was found in the retained main log; absence is not proof of no loss.
No evidence here establishes a camera/LiDAR extrinsic error or clock offset.
