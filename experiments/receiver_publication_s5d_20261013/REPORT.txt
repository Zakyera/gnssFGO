S5d — What does strict full-history publication refinement actually change?
2026-10-13. Diagnostic only. COMPLETED; no production policy changed.

Conclusion
----------
The slow receiver is doing substantially more work than an ordinary incremental
gnssFGO update: our publication wrapper repeatedly forces whole-history nonlinear
relinearization and QR, then recomputes a fresh whole-graph stationarity test.
The growing graph AND the increasing number of these passes explain the late cost.

The first pass is not dispensable numerical noise: among 18 preselected snapshots
the initial-to-final velocity correction reaches 0.110798 m/s. However, subsequent
passes make much smaller physical changes. After one pass, the largest remaining
position difference anywhere in the retained history is 0.172509 mm; the largest
remaining velocity difference is 0.000734446 m/s. The first pass supplies at least
99.9363% of that publication's total objective decrease at these checkpoints.

Late example: after five passes at 142.4 s only FOUR variable blocks still violate
the strict gradient threshold, but the sixth pass relinearizes ALL 4,275 blocks.
It passes stationarity but triggers a QR recovery because of a tiny raw objective
increase. Recovered and rejected candidate positions differ by only 6.754e-11 m.
This identifies costly global convergence/polishing in OUR wrapper. It does not
prove that removing refinement or loosening its tolerance would be safe.

4. Where the strict certificate is failing
-----------------------------------------
Certificate = max_j |(J^T r)_j| / sqrt((J^T J)_jj), evaluated at current Values.
This is a normalized first-order stationarity check, not a physical pose-error
bound, a calibrated uncertainty, or a guarantee of a global optimum.

Only 13/1480 ordinary support estimates already pass 1e-5 (the final publication
also starts stationary). At 40 s, 183 of 1203 blocks have violating coordinates;
the strongest is W341, age 5.9 s. At 120 s, the strongest is pose X1109 rotation,
age 9.1 s; current V1200 also has normalized gradient 3.55353. At 148 s, the
strongest is current V1480 component 2, value 6.42623. These are not solely
ancient inactive states. The strongest errors can be current or recent-history.

Mechanism in exact code:
- DiagnosticReplay.cpp::RefinePublication calls numerical_audit::Refine.
- FullPublicationRefinement.h::Refine first uses calculateEstimate(), full error
  and fresh NormalizedGradient. Each trial sets force_relinearize=true AND
  forceFullSolve=true, updates an iSAM2 copy, obtains calculateBestEstimate(),
  then evaluates the full nonlinear objective and fresh Jacobians again.
- Pinned GTSAM ISAM2-impl.h:386 uses CheckRelinearizationFull(delta,0.0) when
  forceFullSolve=true (commented 'for debugging'), before fixed/noRelin exclusions.
  Thus this flag does more than request full linear back-substitution.
- NormalizedGradient in offline_process/BoundedISAM2Refinement.h computes current
  graph.linearize(values), gradientAtZero and hessianDiagonal. Checking a scalar
  tolerance therefore triggers real full-graph derivative work.
- The older S5c measured profile attributes 79.52% overall CPU / 88.18% late CPU
  to publication; source derivatives and QR dominate its disjoint kernels. These
  are prior profile percentages, not freshly measured percentages after E2b.

solver_->update then calculateEstimate, not our extra strict-refinement wrapper.
Their IncrementalFixedLagSmoother removes old states by lag. Our receiver retains
all history and performs high-rate stable-ID source revisions. Those workloads
are different. Local upstream comparison used upstream/ros2 revision

6. What is resolved, and what remains
------------------------------------
Resolved: the expensive passes do not correspond to large pose changes on every
iteration. They provide one substantive correction, followed by small physical
refinements and sometimes numerical recovery. The wrapper's global scheduling
makes even a handful of remaining recent-state violations pay whole-history cost.
This is a concrete engineering target, not evidence that WNOA itself is invalid,
that covariance must change, or that ROS/Rerun/GPU transfer is this bottleneck.

NOT resolved: a replacement production policy. These snapshots all inherit the
previous publication's FULLY refined history. They cannot predict accumulated
error if we stop early at every future update. No tolerance, iteration cap,
relinearization rule or covariance should be changed from this diagnosis alone.
The original small live/offline mismatch remains unresolved; the exactness here
is relative to the established OFFLINE oracle, not claimed live reproduction.

One narrow next decision, NOT executed: separate incomplete linear propagation
from necessary nonlinear relinearization. ISAM2.h:210–248 documents that
calculateEstimate uses wildfire-limited delta, whereas calculateBestEstimate
performs full back-substitution. On an isolated copy of selected existing solver
states, compare full back-substitution ONLY with the current initial estimate
and first global pass, with the same fresh gradient and physical metrics. Do
not run another source sequence, publish the trial, drop the global gate, or
repeat E4 blindly. If it remains nonstationary, that cheap completion is not a
solution; further policy work needs an explicit bounded-cost/error contract.
This S5d observer alone cannot separate those effects because the first trial
changes both relinearization and linear-solve completeness.
