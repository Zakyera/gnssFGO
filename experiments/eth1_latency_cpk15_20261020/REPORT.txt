BOTTOM LINE
===========
There are TWO different failures. Simply slowing to0.10x is not a solution
to both:

1. CP-K13 at0.30x: real GLIM computation/queue delay exhausted the receiver's
   retention margin. GLIM MAIN GPU-factor linearization is overwhelmingly the
   expensive stage. A concrete build integration defect was identified:
   the loaded ROS bridge's GPU linearization-hook registration is compiled out.

2. CP-K14 at0.20x: the first rejected revision is KIMERA, not GLIM. Its source
   window spans11.10 physical seconds. The receiver retains7 seconds. A5.10s
   gap between keyframes expands Kimera's count-based window beyond that limit.
   This is a lifecycle/retention contract mismatch, not a slow Kimera backend
   or an overloaded gnssFGO queue. Slower playback does not shorten it.

No timing result here establishes an accurate or healthy complete OFF/ON pair,
a safe universal playback rate, or statistically calibrated covariances.

are represented as unary factors once outside the optimized window.
ISAM2 relinearization work also varies by update. Bounded poses do not mean
constant factor count or a constant number of linearizations.

E. RECOMMENDED NEXT ACTIONS — NOT IMPLEMENTED
============================================
Do NOT launch another full run at0.10x as the next diagnostic.
Do NOT change WNOA Qc, covariance scales, factor weights, Kimera keyframe rules
or numerical guards to conceal either failure.

Priority1: make the revision/retention contract explicit.
Receiver retention must cover each source's oldest still-revisable physical
endpoint, delivery/processing delay, and the needed interpolation bracket.
In shorthand:
  retained history >= source revision horizon + delivery age + bracket margin.
Kimera's25-state horizon does not provide a5s time bound.

The correct design discussion is explicit source finalization/watermarks and
receiver retirement, with a separately specified maximum retained history and
late-source/backpressure policy. Pinning every live factor forever is not a
bounded solution. Nor is silently discarding revisions after their previous
version was absorbed. A fixed larger lag would cover this particular11.13s
event, but choosing12s from one capture would NOT prove a general contract.
Potential alternative: an explicitly bounded source revision-export policy;
that intentionally changes which revisions the downstream graph receives and
requires a declared preservation comparison. No choice is silently made here.

Priority2: repair MAIN GPU-hook build integration in an isolated candidate.
Use the existing gtsam_points batching facilities; no new solver is needed.
Verify the hook is actually registered before constructing the main smoother,
assert effective runtime registration/cache hits rather than trusting a log
string, and benchmark only a short saved/frozen workload first.

IMPORTANT mathematical caveat: enabling the ordinary native GPU hook is not
automatically byte-identical to the current synchronous path.
IntegratedVGICPFactorGPU::store_linearized() also updates inlier bookkeeping;
store_linearized_preserving_sync()/linearizePreservingSynchronousVGICP() were
already added for the previous LOCAL batching work for this reason.
Check equations, correspondences/inliers, theta/cache lifecycle and outputs
against the frozen synchronous reference before claiming a math-preserving
speedup. Reuse that existing preservation machinery where appropriate; do not
blindly flip a global build flag and call the numerical behavior unchanged.
No speedup factor or validated patch is claimed by this static audit.

Only after those two bounded gates should a fresh matched experiment be
authorized, with a rate chosen from measured producer delay margins. These
are two specific engineering issues, not an invitation to tune three-factor
fusion against ground truth or restart a broad solver investigation.
