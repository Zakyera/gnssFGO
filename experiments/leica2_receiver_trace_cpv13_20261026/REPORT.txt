2. WHICH OPERATION MOVES THE NEWEST ESTIMATE?

Prism-point displacement of the SAME newest state, immediately before/after
each applied source update, in the trace interval. Median / p95, centimetres:

Operation                           CBS OFF            CBS ON
New GLIM belief, 102 updates         3.267 / 6.426      2.081 / 6.505
New Kimera belief, 51 updates        6.455 /12.856      3.723 / 6.944

Thousands of revisions of EXISTING belief IDs generally cause much smaller
individual movements. For OFF the p95 is 0.042cm per GLIM replacement and
0.247cm per Kimera replacement. Their cumulative effect is not zero: net
per-100ms-cycle RMS movement is 0.446cm GLIM and 0.629cm Kimera in OFF,
0.375cm GLIM and 0.574cm Kimera in ON. These are vector magnitudes, not additive
fractions of path length. Stable-ID replacements do not accumulate duplicate
factors.

Retirement/marginalization: newest-state p95 change only about 0.009cm in
either mode; maximum OFF 0.0424cm / ON 0.0323cm. It is not the large instantaneous
jump mechanism in this interval. This does NOT certify the marginal Hessian's
conditioning or exclude an indirect effect of historical information.

Creating a new support: its pose is almost exactly the pre-step WNOA
prediction. Extra adjustment after that prediction has p95 0.00248cm OFF /
0.00195cm ON, maxima below 0.0041cm. The predictor itself advances the newest
point by a median 3.44cm OFF / 1.69cm ON per 100ms. Thus the new-state creation
solver step is not injecting the centimetre-scale discontinuity; the current
position/velocity already carries the source-driven response.

We reconstruct every selected ONLINE step exactly as the vector sum of source
updates, new-support movement and retirement movement. All 100 cycles per mode
close with zero observed double-precision difference; observed publication
positions also equal the original aligned samples exactly.

The 380--390s cycles alternate GLIM-only and GLIM+Kimera. Do NOT translate this
into "Kimera is wrong and GLIM is right": insertion order, different durations,
availability and the pre-existing graph all condition each update. In OFF,
GLIM and Kimera insertion displacements within the same mixed cycle usually
point in the SAME direction (median cosine +0.803); in ON they often oppose
(median -0.745). A simplistic two-sensors-pulling-oppositely story is inadequate.
New-belief insertion is a localized operation attribution, not a counterfactual
proof that removing that source would improve accuracy.

5. WHAT DOES "WEAK PRIOR" MEAN HERE?

A. Starting anchor: initial pose sigma is 1e-5 (rotation radians/translation
metres). It is already tight. Initial velocity sigma10m/s and angular velocity
sigma3rad/s do not repeatedly reset every support. Simply tightening the start
pose is not supported as the repair for this late-run localized behaviour.

B. Marginal prior: summarizes retired information in the original chart. Its
instantaneous retirement update is tiny here, and older states remain stable.
We have NOT measured its normalized condition number or certified stationarity.
There is no basis here for multiplying this prior by an arbitrary weight.

C. Ongoing WNOA motion prior: connects pose/velocity/angular velocity between
supports. This is the relevant candidate if the proposed remedy means resisting
abrupt changes at the newest end. Qc variances remain .00375 rotation / .015
translation; the actual joint discrete Q(dt) is [dt^3/3 Qc, dt^2/2 Qc;
dt^2/2 Qc, dt Qc]. At dt=.1, its pose-residual marginal standard deviations
are about .001118rad and .002236m. These are NOT absolute position uncertainty
or a maximum allowed step; pose and velocity are estimated jointly. Comparing
these numbers directly to an arbitrary source covariance trace is invalid.
