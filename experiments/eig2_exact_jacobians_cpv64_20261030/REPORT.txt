DECISION
========
Correcting the exact derivatives identified in CP63 does NOT materially reduce
the first GLIM-induced response. CBS-only correction reduces the largest old
orientation revision by0.00037193%; pose-prior-only correction by0.03295690%.
Neither reduces the maximum gyro-bias revision. Both are well below the
predeclared1% response-reduction benchmark. The visual support identities are
unchanged, not merely the counts.

These are real native Jacobian approximations, but this controlled experiment
is evidence against either being the main cause of THIS first disturbance.
Do not claim all later CBS events are thereby correct, that combined changes
were tested, or that the full trajectory zigzag is solved.
Stop pursuing these isolated approximations as a promising first-event fix.

CONCLUSION / NEXT DECISION
=========================
This closes a useful narrowly defined question: exact CBS or pose-prior
derivatives individually do not materially change the first harmful response.
Do not promote either private candidate as a zigzag fix or launch a full run
on this evidence. Later events and a combined candidate remain untested, but
these results do not justify chasing this lead further as the main explanation.

What remains supported is the CP52/CP63 mechanism: a short-interval external
rotation disagreement is accommodated by coupled window/gyro-bias changes,
with worse native measurement fit. That does not determine which source is
physically wrong, establish calibrated covariance, or prove that the overall
CBS design cannot work.
The next discussion should distinguish model/information-consistency questions
from implementation faults; no weighting experiment or new capture is launched
or authorized by this report. Stop here for the user's decision.
