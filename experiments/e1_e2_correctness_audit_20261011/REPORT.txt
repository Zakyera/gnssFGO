E1/E2 correctness and integration audit — 2026-10-11

Conclusion
No remaining E1/E2 numerical correctness defect was reproduced in the expanded
tests and matched frozen replay. This is bounded validation, not proof that all
bugs are absent. One E2 implementation inefficiency was confirmed and corrected
in an isolated candidate: duplicate matrix construction/whitening on cache misses.
An execution-environment mismatch in the first comparison was detected, isolated,
and retained, not hidden by loosening the numerical gate.

E1's component acceleration repeats. E2 has a repeatable modest benefit on this
60 s journal: mean process time 59.39084 s OFF vs 56.31101 s ON, a 5.1857% reduction.
Neither result proves sustainable live whole-pipeline throughput. Both candidates
remain default-off; no installed/working receiver or launch profile was replaced.
E1b/E3/E4 and fresh source/live runs were NOT started.

1. What was audited

E1 is GLIM LOCAL covariance-preparation batching, NOT acceleration of the main
or LOCAL sidecar optimizer. E2 is exact same-factor/same-referenced-Values
linearization reuse, NOT selective/stale relinearization or a new optimizer.

  Unexpected/nonfinite results are not cached. Every successful returned cache
  hit is a clone, so caller mutation cannot alter the retained Gaussian.
- clone() copies immutable factor data, not cache state. Revised mean/covariance

The initial E1 prototype DID have a numerical bug (native batching altered
inlier/error-cache semantics). Its rejected fixture/report remain rejected.
This audit concerns the corrected synchronous-compatible implementation; it does
not retroactively declare the original prototype correct.

3. Isolated E2 fix

Old miss path called jac->augmentedJacobian() for allFinite(), then again solely
for .size(). GTSAM's implementation constructs AND whitens a dense matrix on
each call. The candidate stores that temporary once, performs the SAME finite
check and uses its size for bookkeeping. Returned/stored Gaussian factors and
factor arithmetic are unchanged. Original E2 source/binary retained untouched.

Pinned WNOA microfixture median microseconds/call (five samples, 3000 calls each):
                           original E2   corrected E2
native computation          13.5801       13.9599
wrapper cache OFF           13.6248       13.7524
alternating Values/misses   14.1362       14.1153
repeated Values/hits         0.1295        0.1356

The tiny miss-time change is below the variability of this small test; no
standalone speedup is attributed to the fix. Hits are cheap but most calls miss.
The complete replay OFF/ON comparison below measures the reuse mechanism as a
whole, not the isolated benefit of removing the redundant whitening.

7. What this means for the unresolved delay

It was too strong to expect E1/E2 alone to eliminate the overall delay. E1
successfully removes a specific preparation cost while main/sidecar optimization
remains. E2 avoids about 15% of derivative calls in this short journal, but still
visits all factors and pays invalidation/signature/copy cost. Most evaluations
are genuinely at changed Values and correctly cannot use this exact cache.
Full-history storage/visits/refinement still grow. These facts explain a modest
gain without requiring a remaining E1/E2 numerical bug. They do not prove a
cause for every historical timing spike or justify beginning a new method here.
