1. Answer

We are not running the same fixed-lag procedure as native GLIM or Kimera.
Those estimators normally perform a small configured number of incremental
updates, retaining the standard fixed-linearization marginal semantics. Our
experimental receiver converts marginals to explicit anchored nonlinear-chart
factors, reloads the retained graph, and requires fresh full nonlinear
stationarity before AND after retirement. It forces relinearization/full solves
while checking every factor. Native fast publication does not demonstrate that
the native graphs satisfy this stronger condition in one or two iterations.

This is a real architectural mismatch, not merely the setting "lag = 7 s".
It explains where the repeated work is introduced. It does not by itself prove
that the chart adapter is mathematically wrong or that waiving the gate is safe.
FL3f already demonstrated that stopping early within this modified procedure
changes the current result and subsequently absorbed prior materially.

The difficult retained objective also really converges slowly under our GN
procedure. It is not just expensive marginalization or a misleading timer.
The previously recorded 116-step solve has a poorly conditioned, nearly rigid
whole-window motion coupled to the old marginal boundary. New saved-objective
diagnostics support significant residual/nonlinear curvature along an observed
late oscillation direction. WNOA contributes, but is not the sole contribution;
the marginal's OWN nonlinear curvature is small in this direction. Thus
"WNOA is broken" and "the marginal chart derivative is wrong" are not justified.

5. Is WNOA the difference? What is and is not proved

WNOA is nonlinear on SE(3), with world V/body W and an asynchronous interpolated
relative-factor model. It is not a scalar constant-velocity line in Euclidean
space. GPWNOAPrior.h:183-197 uses Log(T1^-1*T2), pose-dependent conversion of V
and the inverse right Jacobian. Native GLIM/Kimera instead have their own IMU
and raw-sensor factors, and different state structure. Their whole objectives
are not interchangeable controls for one WNOA factor.

However, the same WNOA equations were used in the full-history reference and
fixed-lag receiver. The large iteration increase is associated with changing
the historical inference and marginal/publication treatment, not introducing
WNOA for the first time. Full history retains nonlinear historical factors and
states; fixed lag replaces older information with a frozen Gaussian summary.
They need not have the same optimum, curvature or convergence rate.

Confirmed: excessive work is requested by our repeated-global publication
contract; the current receiver is not stock incremental fixed lag; the saved
retained objective has genuine slow alternating convergence and poor conditioning.
Supported, not isolated proof: frozen-prior anchoring of a nearly common-window
mode plus residual curvature from active WNOA/source factors makes GN slow.
NOT confirmed: a wrong Qc, wrong dt, missing factor, invalid marginal derivative,
or WNOA implementation bug that explains the observed slowdown universally.
No new-source/native timing test was necessary for these conclusions.

6. Engineering implication — proposal only

The next design decision belongs at the marginalization/publication interface,
not a WNOA/Qc/covariance tuning campaign or further blind iteration-limit sweep.
Native-style incremental fixed lag and strict stationarity of our explicit
nonlinear frozen-prior objective are DIFFERENT solver contracts. We must choose
and validate one consistently; mixing stock marginalization with a mandatory
batch-like postcondition created the current custom adapter/refinement path.
If adopting native semantics, test a genuine stock fixed-linearization receiver
from before retirement, with honest approximate publication labels, exact factor
accounting and explicit bounded state/prior errors against a retained reference.
That is not the already-rejected FL3f shortcut, which kept the custom adapter
and truncated refinement late in its inherited history. It requires separate
authorization and a scientifically defensible acceptance contract; no promotion
or tolerance change is made here. If strict nonlinear stationarity remains a
hard requirement, further solver/coordinate work is needed; native update counts
alone cannot certify that requirement. We have not found a proven one-line fix.
