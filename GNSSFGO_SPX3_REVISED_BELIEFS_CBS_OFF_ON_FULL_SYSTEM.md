# SPX-3 matched classic-CBS full-system revision experiment

Date: 2026-09-27. This experiment compares CBS-OFF and **classic** bidirectional CBS-ON source estimators with a frozen gnssFGO receiver. Source-to-source `live_revision` is disabled. Later current-smoother revisions are exported only for downstream stable-ID factor replacement. No covariance scaling, gating, health weighting, robust loss, or model tuning is part of this comparison.

## Protocol and provenance

The SPX-3 mission is `2024-11-02-17-43-10`, using the same 32.0–166.0 s bag section, Hesai LiDAR, front Alphasense stereo camera, and shared CPT7 IMU in both source modes. The source profile is copied from `runs/classic_cbs_restoration_20260925/spx3_off_native/`; its saved OFF and classic-ON GLIM and Kimera profile trees are byte-identical. The 0.20 s CBS horizon, one horizon pair/update, Schur-relative-between full 6×6 covariance, and persistent external factors are unchanged. Only the ON run receives the classic bidirectional source-to-source factors. Both runs use 0.10× playback so revision diagnostics do not starve the source estimators.

The Noetic source image is `cbsms:noetic-cuda-glim`. Revision export is read-only with respect to the source smoother. A logging-only launch selector routes GLIM ROS output to a per-node log; Kimera's glog audit rows remain in the estimator launch log. Previous combined-console and ROS `rosout.log` attempts are retained but excluded: the former interleaved rows, and the latter dropped high-rate diagnostics. Only completed, complete-width node/estimator logs enter this experiment.

The unchanged receiver executable is `/home/yeranis/workspaces/gnssfgo_ct_ws/build/online_fgo/src/offline_process/grandtour_ct_replay`, SHA-256 `b283fb0d9be9b471e85788a94acc9b308acc2ee53e51219846c3f1f7d80a174e`. It uses full-history iSAM2 with Combined IMU, WNOJ (`Qc=10000`, scale 1), ALL_RIGHT CPT7 integration, existing priors and GLIM endpoint support grid, and native full source covariances (scale 1). The IMU CSV SHA-256 is `c041516c61056977f5b249227e6ab981cd1603e8dcefd19b53523e7f7ff3a7d9`.

For each stable `(source, from_index, to_index)` belief ID, the recorded first publication is matched to the same smoother update's diagnostic mean and all 36 covariance entries. Every later jointly valid active-window revision carries a new mean, a newly computed full covariance, and its causal availability timestamp. The gnssFGO receiver removes the old factor and inserts the new factor for that ID; no revision is sent back to GLIM or Kimera. `online_trajectory.tum` is the as-played receiver state at each support time. `trajectory.tum` is the final retrospectively revised graph estimate over those same support times. This distinction is essential.

The original four cases are A=OFF revised GLIM, B=OFF revised GLIM+Kimera, C=classic-ON revised GLIM, and D=classic-ON revised GLIM+Kimera. Two subsequent controls complete the requested 3×2 comparison: K_OFF=OFF revised Kimera only and K_ON=classic-ON revised Kimera only. They use the same IMU, initialization, support grid, evaluation masks, and graph settings. The `--mode kimera` receiver still loads the GLIM CSV to define the frozen endpoint support grid, **but schedules zero GLIM measurement factors**. Only source mode and the included relative-belief source(s) change.

**Meaning of online here:** the classic CBS-ON GLIM↔Kimera factor exchange happened during the recorded SPX-3 source playback. gnssFGO did *not* co-run live with those estimators. It subsequently replayed the recorded causal belief availability times. Its `online_trajectory.tum` is the as-played causal receiver product; `trajectory.tum` is the final graph after later revisions. Despite the framework name, this experiment contains **no GNSS measurement factors**.

The final graph improves substantially over most online products because later source revisions change historical factors; **K_OFF ATE is an exception** (0.978285 online, 0.991234 final). The original machine-readable [result summary](/home/yeranis/results/gnssfgo_spx3_full_system_revisions_20260927/matched_result_summary.json) covers A–D; the two added controls have their own `summary.json` and identically named evaluator JSONs under `evaluation/`. The completed 3×2 comparison separates three distinct observations. Classic CBS-ON makes **Kimera-only gnssFGO much better**: final ATE 0.991234→0.140866 m and final t-RPE 0.117909→0.016765 m. It does not improve **GLIM-only gnssFGO**: final ATE 0.010562→0.014843 m. And it does not improve the **joint gnssFGO graph**: final ATE 0.054274→0.067920 m. Relative to its own ON GLIM-only control, adding Kimera raises final ATE from 0.014843 to 0.067920 m, although it slightly improves the 1 s t-RPE (0.014935→0.012847 m) and r-RPE (0.617394→0.611380°). Thus the strong Kimera source/receiver gain alone does not imply a joint-graph gain; this matched comparison does not isolate which graph information interaction causes the loss.

## Answers to the five questions

1. **Does Kimera improve CBS-OFF gnssFGO after GLIM preservation is fixed?** Not overall. B versus A increases final ATE from 0.010562 to 0.054274 m (5.14×) and t-RPE from 0.009754 to 0.011785 m (+20.8%); r-RPE improves slightly from 0.284259° to 0.272758°. Online ATE also rises from 0.034775 to 0.064765 m.
2. **How much does native Kimera improve under classic CBS-ON?** ATE falls 0.668695→0.172663 m (−74.2%); t-RPE 0.167949→0.031172 m (−81.4%); r-RPE 0.652596→0.579094° (−11.3%). This is a genuine same-run native-source gain.
3. **Does revised GLIM remain healthy under ON?** Its final gnssFGO GLIM-only ATE is still low in absolute terms (C 0.014843 m), but worse than OFF A (0.010562 m), with t-RPE 0.014935 versus 0.009754 m and r-RPE 0.617394° versus 0.284259°. Native ON GLIM likewise degrades relative to native OFF. Source preservation is much better than the old first-publication representation, but ON is not a GLIM improvement here.
4. **Does complete ON joint graph D improve over matched OFF joint graph B?** No. Final D versus B ATE is 0.067920 versus 0.054274 m (+25.1%), t-RPE 0.012847 versus 0.011785 m (+9.0%), and r-RPE 0.611380 versus 0.272758° (+124%). D versus its own ON GLIM-only control C has much worse ATE (0.067920 versus 0.014843 m), though slightly lower t-RPE and r-RPE.
5. **Is the difference mainly online?** No. D is worse than B online (ATE 0.077994 versus 0.064765 m) **and** in the final revised graph (0.067920 versus 0.054274 m). Retrospective revisions narrow some online error, but do not reverse the OFF/ON joint ordering. The added Kimera-only controls confirm Kimera improves strongly in both products while the joint ordering remains opposite. No causal attribution to Kimera covariance, CBS weighting, or WNOJ is claimed from this six-case comparison alone.
