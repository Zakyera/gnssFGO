## Failure evidence

The Hesai bag itself spans `1730565871.089233`–`1730566096.888473` and contains 2,259 `/boxi/hesai/points` messages. A direct timestamp check found its largest consecutive point-cloud gap to be about 0.1013 s, so the missing GLIM tail is not a missing LiDAR segment. During the initial OFF run, a live `rostopic hz /boxi/hesai/points` check still observed about 10 Hz while GLIM odometry was stalled.

In the initial OFF GLIM log, the last normal `GLIM_ROS_INPUT_TIMING_ROW` showed workload 10 before/after enqueue; the last processed smoother update was ordinal 889. Thereafter `TimeKeeper` repeatedly logged `large time difference between points and imu`, with its last point-cloud stamp fixed near `1730565993.588847` while IMU time advanced to `1730566022.864199` (29.275 s difference). In ON the corresponding fixed point stamp was near `1730565984.388651`, reaching a 38.476 s IMU difference. In the slower OFF retry the last input-timing row again reached workload 10 and GLIM stopped near `1730565993.3`. The source code's `GlimROS::insert_frame()` waits while odometry workload exceeds 10 ([glim_ros.cpp](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim_ros1/src/glim_ros/glim_ros.cpp:299)); `AsyncOdometryEstimation::workload()` counts its queued and internal frames ([async_odometry_estimation.cpp](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim/src/glim/odometry/async_odometry_estimation.cpp:47)). The last async-odometry trace in each capture was near the last processed frame. **The logs establish a GLIM processing/queue stall, but not the deeper reason its odometry worker stopped draining; a deadlock or a particular scan failure is not yet proven.**

The near-same physical stopping point at both 0.10× and 0.05× OFF shows that simply slowing playback did not recover the 120 s source coverage. The ON stall occurred earlier. The slower diagnostic's `max_imu_clock_lag_ns` includes a large pre-bag seed-clock startup outlier after reuse of the ROS master; its saved IMU journal starts at the correct SPX-3 timestamp. This diagnostic was invalid for full coverage independently of that clock warning.

### Follow-up localization of the stall (read-only, 2026-09-29)

The retained traces narrow the GLIM stop to its **factor-graph inspector callback**, not to a missing scan or the gnssFGO optimizer. In each failed capture the final `GLIM_CBS_TIMING_ROW,on_smoother_update_finish` is one pose index beyond the final `GLIM_FACTOR_GRAPH_INSPECTOR_ROW` and final `GLIM_ODOM_IMU_TIMING_ROW,processed`:

| Capture | Last completed CBS smoother-finish callback | Last completed inspector snapshot / odometry frame |
|---|---:|---:|
| OFF 0.10× | 889 | 888 |
| ON 0.10× | 797 | 796 |
| OFF 0.05× | 897 | 896 |

The [GLIM ROS constructor](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim_ros1/src/glim_ros/glim_ros.cpp:109) registers the CBS bridge before the factor-graph inspector. The [callback dispatcher](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim/include/glim/util/callback_slot.hpp:46) invokes registered callbacks in order; these are the only two `on_smoother_update_finish` registrations in the source tree. The CBS callback logged its completion for the next pose, but the inspector's [snapshot callback](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim_ros1/src/glim_ros/glim_factor_graph_inspector.cpp:337) did not reach its end-of-snapshot log. The odometry thread subsequently produced no `processed` row. The next scan therefore remained queued; once its backlog reached 10, [GLIM's input backpressure loop](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim_ros1/src/glim_ros/glim_ros.cpp:299) stopped admitting additional scans even though the bag's LiDAR topic and IMU continued.

All three failed runs enabled the inspector with `stride=1` and `full_history=true` ([resolved OFF parameters](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/runs/gnssfgo_spx3_cbs_off_live120_20260928/spx3_source/resolved_glim_params.yaml:99)); the earlier completed SPX-3 OFF/ON source runs had `glim_factor_graph_inspector_enable: false` ([completed OFF parameters](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/runs/spx3_full_system_revision_diagnostics_20260927/spx3_off_classic_revisions_nodelog/resolved_glim_params.yaml:100)). The inspector's [snapshot path](/home/yeranis/repos/V4RL/cbs_gtsam4.3/src/glim_ros1/src/glim_ros/glim_factor_graph_inspector.cpp:620) synchronously emits Rerun geometry on the estimator thread, including the accumulated trajectory when full history is enabled. This is a strong explanation for why recording the requested graphs can stop the source, but the retained logs have no stack trace or in-function timing markers: they **do not prove** whether the blocking operation was Rerun transport/backpressure, graph traversal, allocation, or another call inside `publishSnapshot()`. There was no logged GLIM exception or out-of-memory error. Slowing playback from 0.10× to 0.05× did not remove this callback-local failure.

Thus the answer to “was it too heavy?” is: **the synchronous graph-visualization path is implicated; general machine or gnssFGO compute overload is not established.** No estimator was rerun and no code or settings were changed in this follow-up.

### Live fusion versus deferred visualization (2026-09-29)

The attempted receiver was concurrent: `launch_grandtour_live_receiver.py` started the persistent iSAM2 executable with `--event-stream` and a FIFO before source playback. `grandtour_live_receiver_ros1.py` subscribed to CPT7 IMU and polled newly completed source revision log lines during playback (10 ms nominal polling sleep). It forwarded their relative means, full covariances and physical endpoints immediately as observed; it did not wait for completed source trajectory files. The adapter also published `/gnssfgo/pose` and `/gnssfgo/path` during playback. Classic CBS is bidirectional between GLIM and Kimera; downstream gnssFGO receives from both without feeding its estimate back into either source. Reading an incrementally growing log is the current live transport, with real flush/poll delay; replacing this with dedicated revision messages would be a separate transport improvement.

A read-only join of `receipts.jsonl` event index with `receiver/stream_progress.csv` event count minus one had zero arrival-timestamp mismatches. Over the first 75 seconds of observed ROS time (before either GLIM stall), belief receipt-to-receiver-processing-completion wall delays were:

| Capture at 0.10× playback | Belief rows | Median wall delay | p95 | Maximum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OFF | 43,403 | 0.094361 s | 0.394217 s | 2.051126 s |
| ON | 43,639 | 0.115174 s | 2.390921 s | 14.802188 s |

These include transport queuing and processing following adapter observation, not the upstream estimator computation or logger flush delay. For a factor waiting for a future support, processing completion means acceptance into the pending schedule, not necessarily optimization of that factor. Support-creation work is measured indirectly through subsequent-event delay. Therefore these are evidence of concurrent processing and transient backlog, not a complete end-to-end latency certificate or 1× throughput claim. The ON backlog spikes warrant separate monitoring even after the inspector stall is removed.

The existing no-viewer launch controls are `CBSMS_PRESENTATION_ENABLE=false` and `CBSMS_FACTOR_GRAPH_ENABLE=false`, with receiver `--graph-snapshots` omitted. Keep the existing full-belief revision diagnostics enabled: they are the current live receiver's input. Keep Kimera's ROS visualization/output pipeline settings intact; disabling only the dedicated Rerun graph inspectors does not require disabling native odometry publication. The next acceptance check is complete matched 120 s source coverage, live fused poses throughout, bounded receiver processing/output delay, and complete revision accounting. This configuration has not been rerun as part of this investigation.

Native trajectories, causal fused trajectories, revision events and the final fused solution can be rendered afterward from the recorded live outputs. Exact historical source factor graphs need snapshots of their then-active states and factor keys/types; trajectories or a later optimizer rerun cannot reconstruct those snapshots faithfully. The current GLIM inspector directly constructs and logs Rerun geometry on the estimator thread and has no inspected record-only switch. Preserving its full graph views without this coupling requires a bounded snapshot handoff to a separate file writer/renderer (with explicit visualization-drop accounting); that change has not been implemented here. Fusion must continue during the source run regardless of when the pictures are rendered.

## Acceptance decision and cleanup

The requested 120 s matched comparison **did not pass source-coverage validation**. No native or fused ATE/RPE numbers are reported for these captures, and the prior 30 s results are not extrapolated to 120 s. In particular, do not interpret a late fusion trajectory after GLIM's disappearance as continuous GLIM+Kimera fusion. The initial OFF source `complete.marker` is insufficient on its own: it coexists with a roughly 30 s GLIM tail gap. The ON source runner correctly rejected its own receiver-tail coverage.

The source runner, bag playback, ROS master, and both containers were stopped after the captures. No estimator/fusion implementation was modified and no performance tuning was performed. The existing worktrees contained unrelated research changes before this attempt; this report and the two run-specific receiver JSON files are the only new repository files for this task. A focused GLIM source-processing diagnosis is required before claiming a valid live 120 s OFF/ON result.
